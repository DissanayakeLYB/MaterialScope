Crystallographers describe the orientation of planes through a crystal using **Miller indices**, written as three numbers in parentheses: `(hkl)`.

The recipe:

1. Find where the plane intercepts the `a`, `b`, and `c` axes.
2. Take the reciprocals of those intercepts.
3. Clear fractions and reduce to the smallest integers.

<Callout type="info" title="Example">
  A plane that intercepts the axes at `2a`, `3b`, and `6c` has reciprocals
  `1/2`, `1/3`, `1/6` — clearing fractions gives the Miller index `(321)`.
</Callout>

## Reading an index

- **`(100)`** — plane intercepts only the `a` axis: a cube face.
- **`(110)`** — plane intercepts `a` and `b` equally: a face diagonal plane.
- **`(111)`** — plane intercepts all three axes equally: the close-packed plane in FCC metals.
