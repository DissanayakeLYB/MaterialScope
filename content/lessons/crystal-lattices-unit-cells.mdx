Solids are either **crystalline** — atoms arranged in a repeating three-dimensional pattern — or **amorphous**, with no long-range order. Most metals and ceramics are crystalline; glass is the classic amorphous material. To describe a crystal we need two ideas: the **lattice**, a repeating array of points in space, and the **basis**, the atom (or atoms) attached to each point. The smallest repeat unit that captures the whole pattern is the **unit cell**.

## Unit cells and packing

A unit cell is described by edge lengths `a`, `b`, `c` and the angles between them. There are seven crystal systems — cubic, tetragonal, orthorhombic, hexagonal, rhombohedral, monoclinic, and triclinic — but three structures account for the vast majority of metals: **body-centered cubic (BCC)**, **face-centered cubic (FCC)**, and **hexagonal close-packed (HCP)**. Two numbers summarize how tightly atoms pack: the **coordination number** (how many nearest neighbors each atom touches) and the **atomic packing factor** (APF = volume of atoms in a unit cell ÷ volume of the cell). The highest possible APF for equal-sized spheres is 0.74.

## Body-centered cubic (BCC)

Atoms sit at every cube corner plus one at the center: 8 corners × 1/8 + 1 center = **2 atoms per unit cell**. Each atom touches its 8 nearest neighbors along the body diagonal, so the coordination number is 8 and the APF is 0.68 — a fairly loose packing. The edge length relates to the atomic radius by `a = 4R/√3`. Examples: α-Fe (ferrite), W, Cr, Mo, V, Na.

## Face-centered cubic (FCC)

Atoms sit at every cube corner and at the center of every face: 8 × 1/8 + 6 × 1/2 = **4 atoms per unit cell**. Each atom touches 12 neighbors (coordination number 12), giving the maximum APF of 0.74 and the relation `a = 4R/√2 = 2√2R`. Examples: Cu, Al, Ni, Pb, Ag, Au, γ-Fe (austenite).

## Hexagonal close-packed (HCP)

The hexagonal unit cell holds atoms at its 12 corners, on its two hexagonal faces, and three inside: 12 × 1/6 + 2 × 1/2 + 3 = **6 atoms per unit cell**. Like FCC, it has coordination number 12 and APF 0.74; the two structures differ only in how the close-packed layers stack (ABAB for HCP, ABCABC for FCC). The ideal ratio of cell height to hexagon edge is `c/a = √(8/3) ≈ 1.633`. Examples: Mg, Zn, Ti, Co, Cd.

## Allotropy: one element, several structures

Some elements change crystal structure with temperature — a phenomenon called allotropy (or polymorphism). Iron is the classic example: **α-Fe (BCC)** below 912 °C, **γ-Fe (FCC)** from 912 to 1394 °C, and **δ-Fe (BCC)** from 1394 °C up to its melting point of 1538 °C. This is far from an academic curiosity: FCC iron (austenite) dissolves much more carbon than BCC iron, which is the entire foundation of steel heat treatment.

The iron–carbon diagram below puts those transitions on one canvas: follow the left axis (pure iron) upward through α, γ, δ, and liquid, and notice how adding carbon widens the γ field dramatically — austenite dissolves up to 2.11 wt% carbon at 1147 °C, while ferrite (α) takes almost none. That asymmetry is where steel heat treatment comes from.

## Worked example: theoretical density of copper

Copper is FCC with an atomic radius R = 0.128 nm and atomic weight A = 63.55 g/mol. Compute its theoretical density.

<details>
  <summary>Solution</summary>

  The density of a crystal is `ρ = nA / (V_C × N_A)`, where n is the number of atoms per unit cell, V_C the cell volume, and N_A Avogadro's number.

  - n = **4** atoms/cell (FCC).
  - a = 2√2R = 2√2 × 0.128 nm = **0.362 nm**.
  - V_C = a³ = (0.362 nm)³ = 4.74 × 10⁻²³ cm³.
  - ρ = (4 × 63.55 g/mol) ÷ (4.74 × 10⁻²³ cm³ × 6.022 × 10²³ /mol) = 254.2 ÷ 28.6 = **8.90 g/cm³**.

  The measured density of copper is 8.94 g/cm³. The 0.4% difference is rounding: R is quoted to three significant figures, whereas X-ray measurements give a slightly smaller lattice parameter (a = 0.3615 nm).

  **Practice:** how many atoms are in one BCC unit cell, and what fraction of the cell's volume do they occupy?
</details>

<details>
  <summary>Answers</summary>

  Two atoms (8 corners × 1/8 + 1 center). They occupy 68% of the cell volume — the APF of BCC is 0.68.
</details>
