Once you know the shape of the unit cell (its crystal system), you still need to know where the atoms sit. A **Bravais lattice** describes the infinite array of points — and therefore atoms — generated by repeating the unit cell.

Combining the seven crystal systems with the possible lattice centerings (primitive `P`, body-centered `I`, face-centered `F`, and base-centered `C`) gives exactly **14 distinct Bravais lattices**.

<Callout type="warning" title="Common mistake">
  It is tempting to think each of the seven systems allows all four centerings.
  In practice many combinations are redundant — the base-centered cubic cell,
  for example, is really just a smaller tetragonal cell in disguise.
</Callout>

## The cubic lattices

The three cubic lattices are the most important for metals:

| Lattice                   | Atoms per cell | Example               |
| ------------------------- | -------------- | --------------------- |
| Simple cubic (SC)         | 1              | Polonium              |
| Body-centered cubic (BCC) | 2              | Iron (α-Fe), Tungsten |
| Face-centered cubic (FCC) | 4              | Copper, Aluminum      |
