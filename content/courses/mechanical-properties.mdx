This course turns the everyday question "how strong is it?" into a quantitative toolkit. You will learn how engineers measure deformation and force, why some materials spring back and others stay bent, and why even strong materials can shatter when a small crack is present. Along the way you will meet the stress–strain curve — the single most useful graph in materials engineering — and use it to design, select, and troubleshoot real components.

<Callout type="note" title="Prerequisites">
  The bonding lesson ("What Makes a Material a Material") is useful context but
  not required. We define every quantity from scratch, and the only math is
  algebra, trig (Schmid's law makes a cameo), and a few square roots.
</Callout>
