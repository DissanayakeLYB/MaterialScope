Solids look frozen, but their atoms never stop moving: every atom in a metal vibrates, and occasionally one jumps into a neighboring site. When enough atoms make these jumps in the same direction, we get **diffusion** — the slow migration of matter through a solid. Diffusion seems glacial at room temperature and explosive near the melting point, and it is the engine behind carburized gears, doped semiconductors, and the creep of turbine blades. This course explains the mechanisms, the two equations that quantify them (Fick's laws), and the industrial processes that exploit them.

<Callout type="note" title="Prerequisites">
  The point-defects lesson is strongly recommended — vacancies are the

  Diagram") provides the context for carburizing, the flagship application.
</Callout>
