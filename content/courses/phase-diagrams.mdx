Alloys are not uniform lumps of atoms — at any temperature and composition they consist of distinct **phases** with different structures, chemistries, and properties. This course teaches you to read the map of those phases: the phase diagram. You will learn the phase rule that governs how many phases can coexist, use the lever rule to compute exactly how much of each phase forms, and finish by reading the diagram that built the modern world — the iron–carbon diagram behind every steel.

<Callout type="example" title="What you'll need">
  You met the Cu–Ni isomorphous diagram in "Point Defects" — this course puts
  it to quantitative use. The crystal-structures course (unit cells, Bravais
  lattices) is good background for the phase names like FCC austenite and BCC
  ferrite.
</Callout>
