Copper wires, glass insulators, and the silicon chips between them: the entire electronics industry is a study in how materials conduct electricity — and how to control it. This course starts from the question "why do some materials carry current easily?" and builds the band model that answers it, then shows how adding a few parts-per-million of impurity to silicon turns a mediocre conductor into the most precisely controllable material ever made. It finishes with the p–n junction, the one structure behind diodes, LEDs, transistors, and solar cells.

<Callout type="example" title="What you'll need">
  The bonding lesson ("What Makes a Material a Material") introduces the

  semiconductors. Some comfort with exponential (Arrhenius) behavior, as in
  the point-defects and diffusion lessons, helps in the middle section.
</Callout>
